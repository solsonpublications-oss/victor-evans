Agent Swarm Build Plan: The Generational Curse Breaker Website
Version 2. A cinematic, dark and light themed author site built around the cover: storm to sunrise, a lit window, a breaking chain. The home page has four parts only: Hero, About the Author, About the Book, Footer.
1. Master prompt
Paste this into your orchestrator agent first. Give every other agent this block, section 2 (Cover DNA) and its own role prompt from section 9. The book file and the cover image are the only sources of truth.
You are the Orchestrator of a build swarm. Build a cinematic, award-level, production-ready author website for Victor L. Evans, author of the nonfiction book "The Generational Curse Breaker: A Family's Battle Against Undue Influence, Systemic Neglect, and the Theft of Carrie Evans's Legacy."

THE FEELING
The visitor must feel the book in their chest within five seconds: a storm clearing into sunrise over a brick Washington row house, one warm window lit, a rusted chain broken on the front steps, a gold title like molten foil. Modern, deep, dynamic, never static. A wow effect on load, on scroll, on hover and on theme switch, without ever feeling cheap or gimmicky. The tone stays dignified: this is a true story about a disabled sister, lost brothers and thirty years of fighting.

HOME PAGE = EXACTLY FOUR PARTS, NOTHING ELSE
1. Hero (one full viewport, cinematic)
2. About the Author
3. About the Book
4. Footer
The home page has no other sections. A slim sticky header (logo, links, theme switch) is navigation, not a section. The five detail tabs (The Timeline, The Family, The System, The Six Demands, Read Inside) live on their own routes under /record and are linked from the header and footer.

THEMES
Two fully designed modes, Dark ("Storm") and Light ("Dawn"), with a system-preference default, a manual switch, no flash of wrong theme, and a signature animated transition between them. Both modes must feel premium, not an inverted afterthought.

SOURCE ASSETS
The cover image (generational_cursebreaker.jpeg, 1024x1536) is supplied. The author photo is not: build a refined placeholder that is swapped by changing one file path.

NON-NEGOTIABLES
- Never invent facts, dates, quotes, reviews, endorsements, ISBNs, prices or retailer links. Unknowns become clearly marked placeholders logged in /docs/OPEN-ITEMS.md.
- Copy must match the manuscript's voice (see Voice rules in section 8).
- Never publish the author's home address or phone number from the copyright page.
- Performance, accessibility and reduced-motion rules in sections 6 and 7 are hard gates, not suggestions.
- Sensitive-content rules in section 8 apply to every agent.

PROCESS
Run the phases and gates in section 9. Enforce file ownership. Return: the repository, /docs/OPEN-ITEMS.md, a QA report with Lighthouse and axe results, and a 60-second screen recording script of the site's best moments.

Typography (two families, about 90 KB total, self-hosted WOFF2, Latin subset)
• Display: Fraunces variable (heavy optical sizes at 800 to 900) for the title and section heads. It carries the cover's weighty, slightly vintage serif in a modern, soft-cut form. Alternative: Recoleta-style or Roslindale if licensed.
• Body and UI: Inter variable, 400 to 600. Eyebrow labels in small caps with wide tracking.
• Fluid scale with clamp(). Hero title around clamp(2.8rem, 9vw, 8rem), text-wrap: balance, tight leading.
• Fallback stacks: Georgia, serif and system-ui, sans-serif, with size-adjusted fallbacks to prevent layout shift.
Theme switch (a showpiece, not a checkbox)
• Default follows prefers-color-scheme; the choice is stored and applied by a tiny inline script in the head so there is no flash.
• The toggle is a sun and moon that morph into each other. On click, the new theme expands as a circular reveal from the toggle using the View Transitions API (fallback: a 250 ms crossfade).
• In Dark the hero sky is a night storm with the window lit; in Light it is a golden-hour dawn, the window glow becomes a warm reflection, and the Capitol silhouette catches the sun. Same scene, two moods.
• All colors, shadows, gradients and the SVG scene colors come from CSS variables so the swap is instant and consistent.
Surface language: frosted glass cards (backdrop-filter used sparingly and only on large static surfaces), 1 px gold hairline borders, soft inner glow on hover, fine film grain overlay at 4 percent opacity (one tiled 4 KB asset), generous 12-column grid, 70-character reading measure.
4. Home page, scene by scene
One long cinematic scroll with four parts. A slim sticky header floats above it: gold diamond-window logo, links (Author, Book, The Record, Contact), theme switch, and a "Get the Book" button. The header turns to frosted glass after the first scroll.
4.1 Hero (one viewport, the first five seconds)
Intro sequence (first visit only, under 1.4 seconds, skippable, never blocks content):
1. Black screen. A single gold chain link draws itself with a stroke animation.
2. The link cracks, a flash of light, and a burst of ember particles floats upward.
3. The storm sky fades in, the window in the row house lights up with a slow breath, and the title assembles.
Return visits and reduced-motion users skip straight to step 3 as a short fade.
Scene layers (parallax, pointer and device-tilt reactive):
• Sky: slow-moving storm clouds (shader or layered AVIF), with rare, soft lightning flashes in Dark mode only.
• Giant silhouette: the faceless watching figure drifting in the clouds.
• Horizon: Capitol dome and park lamps, brightening as you scroll.
• Row house: inline SVG brick silhouette with the lit window; the window glow pulses like a heartbeat and brightens when the cursor approaches.
• Foreground: steps and the broken chain with a gold glint that travels across the links.
• Particles: embers rising (Dark) or golden dust motes (Light), canvas 2D, capped at 60 particles.
• Rain streaks in Dark mode, off on mobile low-power devices.
Content:
• Eyebrow: A true account. First edition, 2025.
• H1 The Generational Curse Breaker in animated gold foil. Words rise in on a mask; "Breaker" gets a kintsugi crack that draws across it and fills with light.
• Subtitle: the full book subtitle, fading up line by line.
• Hook line in the author's voice: "I am not asking you to take my word for it. I am asking you to read the record." (verify wording against the manuscript before use)
• Buttons: Read the Record (goes to /record) and Get the Book (placeholder link). Both magnetic: they lean toward the cursor, with a light sweep on hover.
• The 3D book: the supplied cover on a floating hardcover with spine thickness and page edges (CSS 3D), tilting with the cursor and with a gold specular sheen that follows it. Click or tap flips it to show the back cover placeholder.
• Scroll cue: a vertical thread with a traveling gold diamond. At the bottom edge, a slow ticker of short phrases from the book: Read the record. Thirty years. The walls remember. I didn't forget.
4.2 About the Author
Pinned scroll scene, about 150 vh tall on desktop, a normal stack on mobile.
• Portrait: a 4:5 frame with a placeholder portrait. The image reveals through a slow ink-bleed mask wipe, then settles with a subtle parallax and a thin gold offset frame that draws itself.
• Text: the author's own words about himself, set large. As the visitor scrolls, the paragraph illuminates word by word from dim to full brightness, scrubbed to scroll. Facts only, from the Author's Note: retired U.S. Army veteran, two master's degrees (Public Administration and Human Resources Management), self-described "not a professional writer," a career among records and accountability, writing because time is running out.
• Numbers strip: four counters that roll like a mechanical odometer when they enter view: 30 years of documentation, 7 children in one row house, 6 demands for justice, 12 chapters.
• Pull quote: typed out with a blinking gold caret, then the caret becomes the diamond mark.
• Background: the storm lightens a notch here; the lamp-lit park glow slides in from the right.
4.3 About the Book
The emotional climax. Pinned two-column scene.
• Left, sticky: the 3D book rotating with scroll through four poses: front cover, three-quarter, spine, back. The gold title flares as each pose lands.
• Right, three beats that crossfade as you scroll:
    1. The House. 2905 Mills Avenue, Langdon Park, seven children and one bathroom. The home that was paid off by hand.
    2. The Net. Displacement after a fire, a will signed under duress, a sister isolated, brothers lost.
    3. The Reckoning. Thirty years of letters to agencies that did not act, and six formal demands for justice.
• The chain moment: a full-width gold-lit chain runs behind the text. During beat three the scroll position scrubs the chain apart: links strain, one snaps, sparks fly and the section brightens from storm to sunrise. This is the site's signature moment. Keep it under 2 seconds of scroll distance and give it a one-tap skip for assistive users (it is purely decorative).
• Book structure as hover cards: four glass cards for the four Parts (Foundations and Fault Lines, The Net Tightens, The Era of Isolation and Tragedy, The Fight for Accountability). Hover or focus lifts the card, glows its border and reveals its chapter titles. A link goes to "Read Inside".
• Who it is for (from the Author's Note): families of people with developmental disabilities, attorneys, social workers, judges, legislators, law enforcement.
• Details card: title, subtitle, author, edition, year. Format, page count, ISBN, price and release date are flagged placeholders.
• Buttons: Get the Book and Request a Review Copy (placeholders).
4.4 Footer
• A giant outlined line, "I didn't forget.", fills with gold as it scrolls into view, with a slow light sweep across it. The storm is now fully dawn: a warm gradient sky and the lit lamps all on.
• Link columns: The Record (five inner pages), Press and Media, Contact, Privacy. A contact email placeholder or a short form (no phone number, no home address).
• The theme switch, a small credit line, copyright 2025 Victor L. Evans, and the nonfiction note from the copyright page.
• A "Back to the steps" button: the diamond climbs three animated stone steps while the page scrolls to top.
• A quiet resource line pointing to Adult Protective Services and the Eldercare Locator, with official contact details verified before launch.

Guardrails (hard gates)
• Animate transform, opacity, filter and shader uniforms only. No layout-thrashing properties.
• prefers-reduced-motion: reduce turns off the intro, parallax, shader, lightning, particles, cursor trail, smooth scroll and scrubbed scenes. State changes stay instant and content stays visible.
• No flashing faster than 3 times per second anywhere. Lightning is a slow, low-contrast fade.
• Effects load in tiers: tier 0 (critical, inline CSS and under 25 KB gzipped JS) renders the full hero without effects; tier 1 (smooth scroll, theme transition, tilt) loads after first paint; tier 2 (shader, particles, cursor trail, GSAP) loads only on devices with enough power and no data-saver, and never delays interaction.
• Mobile gets a simplified scene: no rain, no cursor trail, fewer particles, shader replaced by layered clouds, tilt replaced by gentle scroll parallax (device orientation only if the visitor opts in).
• Pause every animation loop when the tab is hidden or the element is off-screen.
• Target 60 fps on a mid-range phone. If a frame budget check fails, the Motion agent must downgrade the effect rather than ship jank.
7. Tech stack, performance budget and SEO
Stack
• Astro static output with TypeScript, plain modern CSS (custom properties, @layer, container queries, scroll-driven animations). No UI framework and no Tailwind runtime.
• Small vanilla JS islands for the theme switch, tilt, counters, header and accordion. Lenis for smooth scroll. GSAP with ScrollTrigger and a tiny WebGL library such as OGL are loaded lazily, only for pinned scenes and the sky shader.
• Images through astro:assets: AVIF and WebP with srcset, explicit width and height, hero image eager with fetchpriority="high", everything else lazy. The scene art is mostly inline SVG and CSS.
• Hosting: Cloudflare Pages or Vercel, Brotli, immutable asset caching, security headers (CSP, X-Content-Type-Options, Referrer-Policy, Permissions-Policy). Optional cookie-free analytics.

8. Voice, legal and ethics guardrails
A cinematic site does not change how the words must sound. The visuals can be bold; the copy stays plain, precise and dignified.
Voice rules (from the manuscript)
• Documentary, not dramatic: a disciplined veteran-administrator speaking plainly, with grief "refined into clarity." Short declarative sentences land after long careful ones.
• Reuse the book's own ledger and home language: record, audit, arithmetic, balance sheet, the steps, the windows, "the walls remember."
• Signature lines available for reuse, all from the manuscript: Read the record. / The Arithmetic of Loss. / This book is the audit. / The walls remember. / I didn't forget. / Hope is a discipline.
• No hype words (explosive, shocking), no emoji, no exclamation points, no invented testimonials, no profanity (the foreword contains a quoted profanity; never surface it).
• Microcopy in the same voice: Read the Record, Open the File, Stand on the Steps.
Legal and ethics rules for every agent
1. Frame allegations as the author's documented account ("the author documents," "according to the book"). Never state them as the site's own findings.
2. Named private individuals: no standalone pages, headlines, meta descriptions or social cards that accuse a named private person of a crime. Accusatory names appear only inside short quoted excerpts, each approved by the Legal and Ethics agent.
3. Phyllis Lorraine Evans is a living person with a disability. No photos, no medical detail beyond what is needed to describe the book, no spectacle. Always "Phyllis," always a sister and a person.
4. Privacy: no home address or phone number for the author or any living person. The Mills Avenue address appears only as the book's historical setting.
5. No invented proof: exhibit-style design is a visual language, not a claim. No fake documents, leaked-evidence graphics or fabricated filings.
6. The silhouettes and faceless figure are artistic devices. Do not add faces or stock photos of real or implied family members.
7. Recommend attorney review of the final copy before launch and log it in /docs/OPEN-ITEMS.md.
8. Care resources: a quiet footer pointer to Adult Protective Services and the Eldercare Locator, with current official details verified before use.
